# GRU Rankings: Initial CE, Learning Progress and Shape Boundaries
## Finding
Default-init GRU64x1 beats post-LN TF32x4 in mean held-out CE across the tested shared low-SGD and weak Adam/AdamW recipes, replicated on eight allowed tables/splits. This is an exact-pair mean-loss tie-break, not GRU-first. Several cells remain statistical ties, and widening the transformer reverses adaptive rankings.
Lower initial CE is directly supported by no-update baselines. It descriptively carries the target pair's low-SGD advantage while the transformer makes more progress. Causal head calibration, logit variance and a recurrent Markov-bias explanation remain unverified; the evidence does not establish calibration rather than inductive bias as the cause.

## Methods and Uncertainty
Only allowed lab datasets were trained: train800/test200 windows, L24, one fixed transition law per dataset. Default family initialization, causal models, dropout0, plain CE, shared batch16, weight decay0. GRU64x1 is nonresidual; TF32x4 is post-LN, GELU, heads2, learned positions. V24 main grid uses FF128; FF64 is a separate sensitivity test. Width/depth audit fixes FF128 and heads2 across GRU32/64 x1/x2 and TF32/64 x1/x4. These shapes are not parameter matched. Initialization conventions are shared within each family; identical numerical weights or minibatch streams across architectures are not assumed.
Main grid crosses plain SGD .0001/.001/.003 with T256/512 and Adam(.9, .999) Delta .01/.0256/.064 with T256/512. Delta=lr*T is nominal exposure, not parameter displacement and not an SGD-to-Adam equivalence. V24 FF64 probes use T256; AdamW FF64 uses Delta .0256, beta2 .999/.95, T256. V32 transport uses FF64, SGD and AdamW(.9, .95), all main-grid points. No continuous-rate cutoff or data-size effect was tested.
Ten seeds were scheduled per configuration. Difference D=CE_GRU-CE_TF; D<0 favors GRU. Pointwise intervals use D plus/minus 2.8*(SD_G/sqrt(n_G)+SD_T/sqrt(n_T)), conservative under approximately normal seed means with n>=9, without assuming paired covariance. They exclude table-population uncertainty, test-window uncertainty and multiple-comparison correction. Shared seeds and repeated budgets are dependent; cell wins are not binomial reliability estimates.
40 saved artifacts contain345 distinct normalized input specifications, 3450 scheduled seed slots, 10 failures and 3440 inferred finite seed evaluations. The only failed endpoint is the step0 capability request, 10/10; all numerical ranking controls have n10/10, not survivor-only means. Cached repeats, ignored-field interventions, AdamW/Adam equivalence at wd0 and no-update budget checks are not independent replications. Schema-validation failures and the >12-variant rejection supplied no seed measurements. Frozen-source strict matching finds0 target-regime exact pairs/0sets; new experiments provide the replication.

## Width and Depth Counterexamples
At T256, SGD .001, all 64 fixed-FF comparisons of four GRU shapes against four TF shapes across four V24 tables favor GRU, including pointwise intervals. At Adam Delta .0256, TF64x4 beats GRU64x1 in all 4 mean comparisons;3/4 intervals favor TF. D values are .016087/.014106 for the two alpha.8 tables and .200641/.152420 for alpha1.4; the first alpha.8 CI [-.004188, .036361] includes equality.
GRU32x1 loses to TF32x4 on all 4 tables, with intervals favoring TF. GRU64x2 versus TF32x4 is tied on both alpha.8 tables and loses on both alpha1.4 tables. TF64x1 also beats GRU64x1 on both alpha1.4 tables, while GRU wins on both alpha.8 tables. Single-layer GRU is consistently better than two-layer GRU under this Adam recipe despite two-layer GRU having lower initial CE. Thus neither the target pair's ranking nor initial CE alone supplies a universal architecture/depth order.

## Causal Tests, Predictions and Boundaries
Predictions were published before each experiment block. Target GRU mean superiority replicated; anticipated weakening at higher shared-SGD exposure appears; widened-TF reversal replicated4/4. The adaptive possibility of a stronger-law target-pair reversal did not occur. A head-scale proxy and position removal were requested separately; a true temperature intervention was unavailable: TF head_scale=.1 and position_embedding=False produced exactly the default mean/SD. These fields are not verified interventions and cannot support a null causal conclusion. The direct T0 request failed10/10. No exposed logits were available; sandbox Torch import was blocked. Retained no-update and default-init results partially resolve measurement access, not calibration causality.
Competing explanations remain initialization dispersion/bias, gradient scale and conditioning, width/depth/parameter count, gating, positions and table/split variation. Preregistered next-epoch discriminator: verified head/temperature calibration reducing the original low-SGD gap by at least 50% supports operational calibration dominance; less than50% attenuation refutes that operational prediction. Retain default initialization and inspect CE0, variance, CE progress and H(Y)-CE separately. A no-position arm must be tested independently, with initial CE matched; a change in post-calibration conditional gain would implicate position/optimization effects, not uniquely Markov bias. Neither test was accomplished here.
Do not extrapolate to V48/L12, equal d64x2, T1024, batch32, new training sizes, other optimizers, decay, momentum, FF/head choices or arbitrary tables. No entropy/alpha threshold is established. For measured recipes, use GRU as a mean-loss tie-break; shared SGD .0001/.001 at both budgets and .003/T256 have clearer evidence than .003/T512. In the adaptive band, FF64 tests are clearer than the alpha1.4 FF128/.0256 near ties. Current K1075's distinct equal-width findings remain intact.
