# Adam-family full-recipe spiral transfer

## Findings
**The historical comparison reverses, and the original recipe reversal transfers at T2048.** Byte-identical choice sections favor A on one turn (`q_fe080b`) and C on three turns (`q_b3f618`). New experiments favor A on both one-turn datasets and C on both three-turn datasets at T2048.

The residual rectifier recipe also beats the plain smooth recipe on both three-turn datasets at T256. This supports the **specific full recipe**, not a universal ≥3× learning-rate preference, residual advantage, or causal turns cutoff.

## Uncertainty and Boundaries
The first hard-T256 margin is small relative to C’s SD. Paired losses/covariance are unavailable; no significance or seedwise win-rate claim follows. Large easy SDs indicate seed-tail sensitivity. Numerically zero A losses require absolute margins, not finite ratios or claims of zero population risk.

The persisted schema retains inherited `lambda10` alongside `loss_id=cross_entropy`. Plain CE was explicitly selected, but implementation-level historical equivalence cannot be independently inspected here.

Faster fitting, decay-limited tiny easy losses, activation/LN interactions, optimizer dynamics and coordinate scaling remain competing explanations. Endpoint CE distinguishes recipe rankings, not these mechanisms.

Unmeasured predictions: a third allowed three-turn split should favor C at both budgets; a third one-turn split should favor A at T2048. Opposite signs reject transfer. Matched optimizer/rate/decay factorials would test whether rate or decay explains the bundle advantage. No extension to all two-turn datasets, activations, architectures or budgets is established.
