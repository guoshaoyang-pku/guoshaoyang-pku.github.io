## Failure and Mechanism Tests
No training-seed failures explain the controlled reversal. Uniform prediction gives ln24=3.178054. Wide RMSprop CE is.064235/.059835 below it; Adagrad is.167407/.075776 below. Narrow RMSprop is.562232/.287076 below. CE proximity does not establish uniform logits, missing token conditioning or calibration.
Archived curves are held-out CE after each update, not training loss. Wide RMSprop mean CE after update1 is3.723659/3.787058; early16-step maxima4.054996/4.144903; minima3.111457 at202/3.117613 at246; endpoints3.113819/3.118219. They show early high loss and little late mean deterioration, not measured parameter overshoot.
Narrow RMSprop deteriorates more from its minima2.522715/2.736562 to endpoints2.615822/2.890978, yet still wins. Therefore late test-CE deterioration alone does not explain the width reversal.
Optimization trajectories, initialization sensitivity, restricted transition fitting, noise/generalization and optimizer state remain competing explanations. Training CE, logits, update norms and actual displacement are unmeasured.

## Predictions and Boundaries
Unmeasured prediction: another allowed V24/L16/alpha1.4 split with this exact recipe should reproduce C/B>1 at d128 and<1 at d64. A reliable opposite sign refutes transfer.
A token-conditioning failure predicts weak input-token information in wide RMSprop logits; near-ln(V) CE with strong conditioning would refute that interpretation. Overshoot requires update/parameter measurements or controlled smaller-rate/clipping rescue. Generalization requires falling training CE while test CE rises; available curves cannot test it.
No universal Adagrad advantage, Delta cutoff, continuous width threshold, optimal rate, alpha law, alternative-initialization/budget/context transfer or original-split numerical reconstruction follows.
