## Trajectories and Mechanisms
Persisted curves measure test CE at every step, **not train CE**. Post-result mean-curve minima for E are .355692/.306975/.286781 at steps94/135/233; for D, .357760/.318238/.303392 at94/132/235. These test-selected minima are descriptive, not valid validation-based early-stopping estimates.

A’s mean CE falls from step512 to1024: .394327→.368741, .372239→.356454, .359086→.338283, improving in30/30 seeds. D rises .630946→.709594, .461737→.543076, .434231→.513786, worsening in8/10,7/10,9/10 seeds. Paired95% intervals for D’s late increase are [.007545,.149751],[-.002950,.165628],[.035881,.123229]; the middle dataset’s late increment remains uncertain.

E’s step512→1024 means are .526381→.563314, .351662→.407636, .399661→.393329. Thus E does not universally worsen through the final half. At T256, both E and D beat A on462152, and E beats A on41478f: **endpoint preference does not extend to every budget**.

Final accuracy A/E/D is .859961/.883057/.883643, .867090/.904883/.908105, and .895264/.918555/.923633. Adaptive recipes classify more points correctly despite worse CE. This is compatible with costly confident mistakes, but does not establish confidence distributions or calibration.

Test-CE degradation is measured. Persistent inability of E/D to reach lower test CE is contradicted by their earlier trajectories. A’s possible underfitting, training-set overfitting, confidence growth, and optimization instability remain competing explanations. Declining full-train CE alongside rising test CE would support fitting/generalization separation; persistently high train CE would support incomplete fitting. Confidence attribution requires logits, calibration, or error-tail measurements. Those measurements are absent.
