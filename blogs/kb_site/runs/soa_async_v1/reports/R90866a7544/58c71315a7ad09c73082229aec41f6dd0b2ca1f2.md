# q_48c5b5: Added Plain Depth Versus Terminal PreLN

## Finding
At the exact original AdamW rate/batch/budget, B beats D on both prespecified two-turn splits and both one-turn boundaries, in 10/10 seeds each. Inserting an unnormalized block **before** the final normalized block also beats D in every seed. Terminal normalization alone does not order these plain networks; its fixed-depth effects depend on depth and dataset.

## Uncertainty, Alternatives And Predictions
Twenty unique cells,200 seed evaluations, zero reported failed seeds/exclusions; every initial cell was uncached. Valid poor fits remain included. Paired intervals assume approximately normal independent seed differences, quantify training uncertainty conditional on fixed splits and have no multiplicity adjustment. Shared candidates make contrasts dependent; four selected splits are not a population sample. Same numeric seeds do not hold head initialization or minibatches fixed across depths.

One-turn losses are tiny: large ratios coexist with sub-.001 margins. Turns, point seed and coordinate range differ together, preventing causal turns attribution. Width128/GELU/plain/default init/.0001 AdamW/T512/b64 is the measured scope; other widths, activations, initializations, optimizers and budgets are untested.

Added nonlinear composition, normalization geometry/affine capacity, head conditioning and finite-budget optimizer behavior compete. R/D rejects the claim that B’s advantage requires an unnormalized terminal block; it does not identify depth mediation. N/B and R/B reject a universal terminal-LN preference here. No feature RMS, gradient, actual update/displacement or scalar-rescue instrumentation was measured; ordinal gold cannot supply it.

Unmeasured prospective test: the third lexicographic allowed two-turn split at the identical recipe should preserve B<D and R<D with ≥7/10 seed wins, while N and R should have higher means than B. Opposite means reject the corresponding transfer prediction. A shared-prefix/head initialization plus independent minibatch-generator replay would distinguish RNG effects from the added-block bundle; width, activation, optimizer and budget sweeps must each be separately preregistered. No broader ranking is asserted.
