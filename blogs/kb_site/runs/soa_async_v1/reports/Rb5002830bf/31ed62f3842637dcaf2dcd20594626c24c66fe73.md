Actual executed model/loss/train/optimizer bytes and measurement SHA256 hashes were verified. ## Mechanism Audit and Distinguishable Predictions
Saved curves contain test MSE at every update, not training MSE, gradients or update norms. The available process instrumentation supports plain cross-entropy/coupled Adam, not these MSE/RMSprop pairs. Training curves and actual updates therefore could not be measured through this interface; no hidden instrumentation is claimed.

Shallow equal-rate early D at step16 is+.224996(P)/+.245073(N), consistent with earlier RMSprop progress. At ratio3 step16 remains+.076990/+.077145, then step64 becomes-.072480/-.090471. At ratio10 step16 is-.269192/-.322495. This fits an early adaptive-scaling explanation for the shallow anchor, without establishing it.

The deep polynomial contradicts a universal RMSprop early advantage: equal-rate D is-1.06120 after one step and-.134716 at16. Deep-N equal-rate also has D=-1.33322 at1. High-ratio first-step losses nearly coincide (absolute mean D<.000021 in all four grids), consistent with the analytical default RMSprop first-step factor near10 versus bias-corrected Adam near1; parameter-update equality was not measured.

Late temporal test-MSE SD over steps961–1024, averaged within seeds, is descriptive motion rather than precision. S/N ratio10 gives Adam .0202694 versus RMS .00504492; D/P equal-rate gives Adam .000463060 versus RMS .00899081. Neither pattern proves optimizer jitter: trend, representation, stochastic fitting and generalization remain alternatives. Test-only trajectories cannot separate training failure from generalization.

Future discriminators, not executed: log full-train/test MSE and actual parameter/function-update RMS at steps1/16/64/256/1024. Early-scaling predicts matched first-motion behavior at ratio10 but a stronger equal-rate RMS kick; a deep-specific function amplification, rather than parameter motion alone, should accompany its early poor test fit. Failure of those signatures weakens this explanation. Late-precision predicts both train/test loss and motion improve after a same-state rate downshift; test-only improvement with worse train fit favors generalization. Preserve weights, moments and future batches in any fork.
