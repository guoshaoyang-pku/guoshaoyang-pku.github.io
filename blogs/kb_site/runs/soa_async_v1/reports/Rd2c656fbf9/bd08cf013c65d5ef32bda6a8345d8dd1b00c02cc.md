# Exact Rectifier Full-Recipe Transfer at T256/512

## Finding
The reversal replicates on the preregistered allowed lab splits at **T512/b32**: shallow residual RMSprop (S) achieves lower ten-seed mean test CE on all four 2–2.5-turn datasets; deep fully normalized AdamW (D) wins both one-turn controls. This is full-recipe transfer, not optimizer-only causality or reproduction of the original benchmark splits. The 2-turn T512 advantage remains uncertain under seed resampling.

## Recipes and Methods
**S:** input Linear(2,192), ReLU, two width192 blocks `ReLU(x + Linear(norm(x)))`, LN `[1,0]`, output Linear(192,2). RMSprop lr `.001`, weight decay `1e-5`, other PyTorch defaults.

**D:** input Linear(2,128), LeakyReLU(.01), five width128 blocks `LeakyReLU(Linear(norm(x)))`, all five LN, no residual, output Linear(128,2). AdamW lr `.0003`, weight decay `0`, betas `(.9,.95)`.

Depth excludes input/head: four versus seven Linear layers. Both use default Linear initialization, cross-entropy, CPU, seeds 0–9, replacement minibatches, 1024 train/2048 test rows, and batch32. T256/T512 budgets are 8192/16384 samples.

All **24 direct cells and eight component cells** were uncached measurements, ten seeds each: **320 outcomes, zero reported failures, errors or exclusions**. Poor finite fits were retained. Generated model, optimizer, loss and training sources were authenticated before component experiments; source files and hashes are archived. Initialization consumes RNG before minibatch sampling, so cross-architecture seed labels do not imply matched minibatches. Dataset materialization was delegated to the allowed lab API.

The prediction that reducing S’s rate harms hard CE passes. The prediction that increasing D’s rate improves hard CE **fails**. Depth signs were exploratory: increasing S depth improves both splits, while reducing D depth worsens both. Thus the recipe result does not establish that shallowness causes the advantage. Depth changes also alter parameter count, initialization/minibatch RNG consumption, and D’s LN count. Easy D-rate differences lie at the numerical floor.

## Interpretation and Boundaries
Endpoint crossover supports differing fitting dynamics, but a scalar lr×T explanation is insufficient: increasing D’s rate worsens hard CE. Conditioning, residual paths, LN placement, activation, width, decay and geometry remain competing explanations. Update norms, parameter motion, training-loss gaps and RMSprop-startup interventions were not measured.

Turns, dataset/table identity, point seed and spatial scale jointly vary. These comparisons cannot identify a causal turns threshold. No exact-recipe 3-turn or beyond-T512 transfer was tested. Hard datasets overlap the motivating base-lab evidence; six selected datasets are not a random population sample. Bootstrap intervals describe observed seed variability, not dataset-general uncertainty.

Unmeasured prediction for a subsequent preregistered test: on another fixed 2.5-turn lab split at T512/b32, S depth5 with LN `[1,0,0,0,0]` will improve mean CE over exact S depth2. Fixed-data coordinate-rescaling tests and crossed depth/rate experiments with controlled minibatch streams would help distinguish competing explanations.
