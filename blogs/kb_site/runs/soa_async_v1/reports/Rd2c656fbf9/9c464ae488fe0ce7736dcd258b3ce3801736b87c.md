# Exact Rectifier Full-Recipe Transfer at T256/512
## Finding
The TRAINING reversal transfers at T512/b32 in the six preregistered allowed lab datasets: shallow residual RMSprop (S) has lower ten-seed mean test CE on all four 2–2.5-turn splits; deep fully normalized AdamW (D) wins both one-turn controls. This is recipe-level replication on different splits, not reproduction of the original held-out benchmark datasets or optimizer-only causality. The 2-turn T512 mean advantage is uncertain under seed resampling.

## Definitions and Methods
S: Linear(2,192), ReLU, two width192 blocks ReLU(x+Linear(norm(x))), LN [1,0], Linear(192,2); RMSprop .001, coupled wd1e-5, other PyTorch defaults. D: Linear(2,128), LeakyReLU(.01), five width128 blocks LeakyReLU(Linear(norm(x))), all five LN, no residual, Linear(128,2); AdamW .0003, wd0, betas(.9,.95). ## Explanations, Limits and Next Tests
Differential fitting dynamics are supported by endpoint crossover, but a scalar lr×T story is insufficient: increasing D rate harms hard CE. Depth can help within either optimizer recipe, opposing a universal shallow advantage. Conditioning, residual paths, LN placement, activation, width, decay and dataset geometry remain competing explanations. No actual update norms, parameter motion, training-loss gap or RMSprop-startup intervention was measured; none is established as the mechanism.
Turns, table/dataset identity, point seed and spatial scale jointly vary. This design cannot identify a turns threshold or distinguish geometric complexity from coordinate scale; no 3-turn or beyond-T512 exact-recipe transfer was tested. Six selected lab datasets are not a random population sample, and the hard datasets overlap the motivating base-lab evidence. Bootstrap intervals describe observed seed variability only; correlation across recipes and heavy tails limit their interpretation.
Unmeasured prediction for a subsequent preregistered experiment: on another fixed 2.5-turn lab split at T512/b32, S depth5 with LN [1,0,0,0,0] will lower mean CE relative to exact S depth2. This is provisional, based on one hard split, and must be tested without changing the rate. Separate fixed-data coordinate-rescaling tests and a crossed depth/rate design with controlled minibatch streams would distinguish scale sensitivity from optimization/depth effects.
