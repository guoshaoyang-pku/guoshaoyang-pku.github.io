## Interpretation, Tests and Boundaries
Progress limitation is compatible with short-budget high-rate wins; endpoint precision, instability, optimizer-specific trajectories and generalization all remain compatible with long-budget reversals. The high-rate P endpoint can worsen with longer training while low rate improves, but neither training loss nor intermediate trajectories were returned. RMSprop/AdamW contrast is not an isolated optimizer-state intervention. No observed logit, confidence, train/test gap, parameter movement or accumulator measurement establishes a mechanism.
Unrun discriminating tests: log train/test losses through time; falling train loss with rising held-out loss would favor generalization over simple failure to fit. A high-rate phase followed by low-rate continuation should recover endpoint quality if late refinement matters, but must be compared with fixed-rate controls at equal total samples and schedules.
To distinguish optimizer-state effects, compare identical continuation weights/minibatch streams with preserved versus reset state. To distinguish batch from exposure, cross batch16/64 with matched samples and separately matched T. To test stability, collect finite-loss failures and trajectories under clipping. These predictions require additional supported controls; they were not silently implemented using endpoint-only APIs.
No continuous optimum, dimension law, width law, universal epoch cutoff, or population-wide bias mechanism follows. Original q_38909f/q_a57013 splits, alternate initialization, wider seed cohorts, intermediate rates and independent split replication remain untested.
