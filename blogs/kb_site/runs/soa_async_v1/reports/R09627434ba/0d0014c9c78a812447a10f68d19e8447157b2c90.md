# Nested Oscillation: Depth, Placement and Budget

Published **R09627434ba**, commit `199967ecb7334f4b1da038e7f8bf9e744c19365d`. No short KB claims were changed.

## Finding
**q_9d5fa6 supports B<A as one recipe-level ordinal contrast, not a general depth law or an isolated added-composition benefit.** Two allowed nested-target transfers reproduce B<A. However, normalization placement explains a substantial portion of the measured contrast, and its magnitude depends strongly on target and training budget. Composition, capacity and optimization remain competing explanations.

## Definitions and Methods
A=plain d2/w64 LN11; B=plain d3/w64 LN101. Controls are C=d3 LN110 and D=d3 LN011. Depth counts blocks after projection. Blocks compute `LeakyReLU(Linear(preLN(x)))`; terminal LN normalizes the final block’s input, not the output head. A has8769 parameters; each d3 model has12929.

All use default PyTorch Linear initialization, Adagrad lr.001/wd0, minibatch MSE, b16, CPU and seeds0–9. Executed-source hashes verify **LeakyReLU slope.01**, including the projection activation, and the actual Adagrad constructor. Unused AdamW betas remain in specification metadata but are absent from executed Adagrad code.

Two allowed targets were selected by expression rather than candidate losses: X=`sym_0c2620`, `cos(2*pi*cos(2*pi*x))-1`, point seed99197854; Y=`sym_1582b1`, `-sin(2*pi*cos(2*pi*x))`, seed32863270. Both have uniform [0,1] inputs and train/test256. “Fresh” refers to this comparison, not previously unmeasured datasets.

Equal LN count and training exposure do not equalize parameters or FLOPs. Same seed labels also do not guarantee identical initialized features or minibatches, because different-depth initialization consumes different RNG draws.

## Interpretation and Distinguishing Tests
LN110 appends an unnormalized block to A and improves both means. Thus moving normalization to the terminal block cannot explain **all** d3 improvement. Nevertheless, the exact endpoint decomposition `B−A=(B−C)+(C−A)` assigns82% of X’s advantage and46% of Y’s advantage to B−C. These are descriptive decompositions, **not causal mediation estimates**.

B/D intervals include zero on both targets. LN101 is therefore not uniquely superior among the tested placements. B/C holds depth, parameter count and activation fixed, isolating a placement intervention at the recipe level. C/A preserves the original two normalized-block positions but still bundles added nonlinear composition, capacity, initialization and optimization changes.

Target shape, offset, symmetry and point split differ between X/Y; their contrasting effect sizes do not identify an offset mechanism. Training-loss decomposition, gradients, actual updates and feature learning were not measured.

Unmeasured predictions and tests remain falsifiable: B<C should persist on another allowed nested split if terminal-preLN placement transfers; an opposite mean sign rejects that prediction. A universal composition claim predicts every equal-LN d3 placement beats A across targets/budgets; any reversal rejects it. Fixed-capacity/FLOP and shared-initialization/minibatch controls are needed. A linear-extra-block control recovering the gain would show that added nonlinear composition is unnecessary.

Conclusions stop at these two fixed splits, w64, default initialization, Leaky.01, Adagrad.001/wd0, b16 and T128–2048. Intervals condition on ten seed labels and fixed datasets, without multiplicity correction. Two selected targets cannot establish population-level accuracy or a universal depth law.
