## Interpretation, Predictions and Boundaries
LN110 appends an unnormalized composition to A; it improves both means, so a terminal-LN change alone cannot explain all d3 benefit. However B−A=(B−C)+(C−A): on X, moving C's second preLN from block2 to block3 accounts for .080409 of the .097637 endpoint advantage (descriptive82%); on Y it accounts for .003019 of .006581 (46%). This is an exact endpoint decomposition, NOT causal mediation. B/D uncertainty includes zero on both targets: LN101 is not a uniquely superior placement.
An extra block changes nonlinear composition, capacity, initialization, Adagrad states and optimization geometry simultaneously. Fixed-depth B/C separates placement from parameter count; C/A retains the first two LN positions but confounds extra composition with extra parameters and changed head inputs. Offset, symmetry, target shape and point split also differ between X/Y; their contrast does not identify an offset mechanism. No train/test decomposition, gradients or parameter motion were measured.
Unmeasured falsifiable predictions: on another allowed nested split, B<C at T512 should persist if terminal preLN placement transfers; opposite mean sign rejects that transfer. A universal composition claim predicts every equal-LN d3 placement beats A across targets/budgets; any reversal rejects it. Fixed-parameter/FLOP, shared-minibatch/initialization and linear-extra-block controls should distinguish nonlinear-composition benefit from capacity/optimization. If a linear extra block recovers the gain, nonlinear composition is not necessary. Long-budget convergence or reversal would challenge a persistent representational-advantage explanation.
Conclusions stop at these two fixed splits, w64/default initialization/Leaky .01/Adagrad .001/wd0/b16/T128–2048. No universal depth, activation, optimizer or population generalization law follows. Intervals reflect ten seed labels conditional on fixed data, with no multiplicity correction; two selected targets do not estimate target-population success rates.
