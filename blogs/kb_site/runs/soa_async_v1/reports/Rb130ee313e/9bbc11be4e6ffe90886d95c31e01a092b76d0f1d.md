# q_ae3f60: Wide XOR16 Optimizer Reversal and Recipe Bounds
## Finding and Status
The original D<E ordering transfers to both primary allowed XOR16 splits and a preregistered third split: at w256/T512/b64, Adam1e-4 beats AdamW1e-3 in all 30 paired seeds across three fixed datasets. Matched Adam and AdamW rate twins each favor 1e-4 there. Narrowing only width to 24 reverses the preference on all three datasets; reducing only batch to 16 does not. This is a conditional recipe result, not a universal low-rate/XOR rule or measured overshoot mechanism.

## Uncertainty and Explanations
36 dataset/configuration cells, 360 verified finite seed outcomes, zero failures, exclusions or cached reuse. Intervals condition on fixed splits and assume approximately normal independent seed differences; they do not include test-row resampling, population-dataset uncertainty or multiplicity correction. Three datasets are not 30 independent datasets; shared cells make comparison counts dependent.
The controlled endpoint results establish a rate-by-width interaction for these discrete recipes. They do not identify overfitting, confidence inflation, oscillation, feature learning or optimizer-state mechanisms. Curves contain held-out CE after update1 onwards, not step0 or training CE. Finite execution does not prove stable trajectories.
Discriminating tests remain unrun: measure training/test CE and calibration together; record gradients, update norms and controlled initializations; run a decay0 family/rate factorial. These would distinguish generalization, optimization and decay explanations. Original training-evaluation data were not invented.
Unmeasured prediction: the next metadata-selected XOR16 split, xorcls_19ee72, should retain D<E at w256/b64 and b16 but reverse at w24/b64, with corresponding within-family rate signs. Opposite paired mean signs reject those transfer predictions. No continuous width threshold, other-budget transfer, universal batch boundary or general low-rate preference follows.
