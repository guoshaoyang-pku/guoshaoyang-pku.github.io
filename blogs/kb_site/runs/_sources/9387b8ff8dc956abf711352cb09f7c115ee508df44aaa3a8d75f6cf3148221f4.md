## Fitting Versus Overshoot
Measured test curves favor faster early held-out fitting by RMS at3e-5:bca step16 R/A=.1291/.5697,step128=.01755/.05870;Adam subsequently overtakes byT512. A052 step16=.8203/2.6644,step128=.4659/.4804,then near-ties atT512. This is observed held-out fitting,not proof of spectral acquisition or accumulator causality;step0 was not recorded.
Bca RMS1e-4/T512 last64 mean=.005139 versus endpoint.000929,within-seed temporalSD averaged.01028 versus Adam.000142. High-rate RMS has larger late variation than low-rate RMS here,but still better mean endpoint than low rate. A052 high-rate RMS also varies more while its endpoint improves. Thus fluctuation alone cannot identify harmful overshoot or prescribe lower rates.
Competing explanations:optimizer transient/state,rate-dependent late noise,initialization/LN interactions,target offset/envelope,decay/betas and generalization. Underfitting predicts poorer TRAIN and test residuals;reversible overshoot predicts reduced late update/loss variance and improved BOTH residuals after a paired downshift;generalization predicts better train but worse test fit. Test curves cannot distinguish these without train loss,per-layer updates,outputs or checkpoint/state forks. These diagnostics are not exposed;no unsupported target transform,external data or surrogate training was used.
