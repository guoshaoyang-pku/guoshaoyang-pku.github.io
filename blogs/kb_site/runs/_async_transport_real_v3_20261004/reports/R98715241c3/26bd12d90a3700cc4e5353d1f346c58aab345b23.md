## Measurements and Methods
The user-reported K1006 utility is −0.07 over 33 uses. Visible explicit citations cover only eight records, with mean score .28125. That score is neither utility nor a reconstruction of n=33.

Lab data contain 12,642 candidates across 1,999 sets and 1,848 datasets. All 10,622 MLP candidates specify default initialization. Named questions use PyTorch Linear defaults and ten seeds, 0–9. Classification datasets have 1,024 training and 2,048 test rows; q_ca0dea has 256/256.

Depth denotes repeated hidden blocks after the input projection. “Terminal LN” means pre-LN before the final hidden Linear, not output-head normalization. Residual blocks compute `act(x + Linear(norm(x)))`; plain blocks compute `act(Linear(norm(x)))`.

Calibrated delta follows K1001/K1019: Adam/AdamW/RMSprop `lr*T`; Adagrad `2*lr*sqrt(T)`; SGD `lr*T*g/(1-m)`, with g=.01 except momentum-.9 regression, g=.03. Delta is a proxy, not measured parameter displacement. The q_ca0dea `.03` observation is raw `lr*T` in its Adam-family stratum, not a universal SGD/Adagrad cutoff.

Strict lab comparisons stay within one set and match architecture, full optimizer, budget, loss and initialization, varying only the factor under examination. Nonfinite/nonpositive losses and failed/excluded candidates are removed. Looser XOR comparisons deliberately match optimizer type/lr while allowing architectural confounding. Historical controlled results below are attributed comments, not rerun experiments; their exact initialization and seed pairing are not recoverable from the comments alone.

Code, pair-level results, complete named records, compact history, frozen KB and complete family-split lab snapshots are saved under `research/`. `research/reproduce.py` reruns the lab and additive audits offline. Reproduction passed.

## Systematic Bias
K1006 states its additive score before its conditional overrides. K1015’s NEVER instruction contradicts K1061 and normalized-terminal-block XOR evidence. K1022 applies a shared `.1` placement boundary across families and makes placement subordinate to the additive hierarchy that failed.

The historical pooled arch-only hit rate, 74/110, including spiral 12/16 and tabular 17/32, is predictive association across heterogeneous choices. It does not identify causal effects of adding LN or residuals.

Current architecture-only history gives the following ordinal accuracy for the literal additive score, excluding tied scores and unidentified ordering between losing choices:

These are dependent ordinal comparisons, not full-ranking hit rates, utility estimates or a replication of 74/110. They preserve useful regression signal while exposing high-delta XOR/tabular failures.

Strict low-delta lab comparisons favor more LN in univariate regression 89/105 across 5 datasets, median MSE ratio .313; multivariate regression 124/141 across 4 datasets, .732; XOR 100/167 across 6 datasets, CE ratio .947. Splitting plain/residual status gives uni 67/75 and 22/30; multi 74/81 and 50/60; XOR 42/70 and 58/97.

Matched residual twins win uni 18/26 across 3 datasets, median ratio .780; multi 31/37 across 3 datasets, .958; XOR 44/77 across 4 datasets, .971. These differing magnitudes and explicit counterexamples do not support residual as a fixed additional LN point. High-delta more-LN wins remain uni 218/330 across 4 datasets, ratio .552; multi and spiral are each only 0/2, too small for general reversals.

## Explanations and Boundaries
Terminal scale restoration could explain plain-network rescues; radial-information loss could explain spiral LN-by-residual sign changes; feature persistence or held-out overconfidence could explain multivariate/tabular failures. None was directly instrumented. Width, activation, target difficulty, batch/epochs and selection bias remain competing explanations.

K1033 already places shallow tabular reversal near delta .03–.1 at 32 epochs. K1009’s opening `.1` rule and K1020’s opening LN-FIRST instruction still risk overriding their conditions. These external conflicts are flagged, not broadly rewritten.

Prospective, untested predictions: with architecture and initialization controlled, terminal-normalized plain XOR input4 should more often beat residual at delta .02 than .10; a new 1.5-turn GELU spiral at delta .0256 may reproduce opposite one-to-three-LN effects in residual versus plain; a new multivariate target at Adam delta .06 should not show a reliable late-LN advantage. Appropriate tests are paired ten-seed interaction cells across independent datasets. Existing counterexamples suffice to remove unconditional instructions; new mechanisms and thresholds need replication.

Pairs share seeds, sets and datasets. Seed-level covariance is unavailable; tiny ratios near .99–1.01 do not establish significance. Findings are bounded to the observed synthetic datasets, MLP conventions, default initialization and T256–2048 budgets. Post-rewrite utility improvement remains unmeasured.
