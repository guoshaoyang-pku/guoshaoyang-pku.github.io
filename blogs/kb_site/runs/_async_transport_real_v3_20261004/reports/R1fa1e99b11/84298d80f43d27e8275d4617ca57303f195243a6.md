## Definitions and preregistered methods
Delta=lr*T is a nominal optimization budget, NOT measured parameter or function displacement. All new runs use allowed lab datasets only, plain MLP, PyTorch-default initialization, Adam betas(0.9,0.95), weight decay1e-5, MSE, batch32, ten seeds. Primary ratios are mean test MSE(deep)/mean test MSE(shallow), reported per dataset, activation, LN and schedule. Width matched factorial: widths24/32/48, depth1/2/4, zero/all pre-LN, ReLU/GELU/SiLU. Requested sweep lr=0.0001/0.0003/0.001/0.003, T=256/1024/2048. Exact matched nominal-budget alternatives will be distinguished from the rectangular sweep.

The exact q target is absent from the allowed lab list. Closest convex control is x**4; nonmonotonic controls will be selected from allowed expressions before observing new measurements. This bounds extrapolation to q_1e921c.

## Predictions before experiments
Overshoot hypothesis: reducing lr should disproportionately rescue deep/all-LN high-lr final loss; at equal lr*T, more smaller steps should beat fewer larger steps. A crossover shared across activations/targets supports an optimizer regime, while activation-specific or target-specific effects weaken a universal reversal. Conditioning/representation hypothesis: architecture and activation contrasts persist at small lr and longer training and differ with target shape. Static final loss alone cannot distinguish irreversible representation limits from optimization failure. Complexity heuristic prediction: oscillatory/nonmonotonic targets favor normalized depth more consistently than simple convex targets, evaluated on held-out allowed training instances after development controls. No general reversal is assumed.
