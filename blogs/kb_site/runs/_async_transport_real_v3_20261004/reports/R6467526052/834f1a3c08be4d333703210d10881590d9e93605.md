Research checkpoint (observations pinned e0002): K1075 should be narrowed, not treated as an equal-width architecture rule.

Question and scope. This review concerns fixed-law bigram LM training only. Test metric is mean held-out token cross-entropy over 10 seeds; lower is better. Comparisons are interpretable only when materialized set, optimizer (including decay/betas), loss, initialization/model schema, width, depth, and training budget are matched. Delta=lr*T is not measured displacement.

Measured phenomena. Later q_9232f7 at AdamW 3e-5, 2048 steps, d_model=64 reports a one-layer GRU beating transformer d_ff=64 and 128; the reported gap is 0.06144 CE. This contradicts an unqualified transformer preference in K1075's 0.02-0.1 band. The audit of 11 supporting lab comparisons is conditional: every transformer had d_ff/d_model >=4, unlike q_9232f7's ratios 1-2. q_2ff577 independently favored a one-layer GRU over a narrow-FF transformer at the same nominal rate/budget, while larger FF won 4/4 matched lab and 2/2 corpus comparisons reported in that audit. q_9344eb separately found GRU64x2 losing to a transformer as small as d_model=32,d_ff=256, so depth and FF cannot be collapsed into “GRU versus transformer.” q_7dbb0e's high-Delta GRU reversal is conditional: later controls found .003 GRU64x2 wins weak rivals at T=256 but loses most at T=1024; no long-budget lab pairs establish a universal penalty. Alpha=1.0 versus 1.4 history contrasts change model, decay, width and seeds, so no alpha threshold is identified.

Lab verification. In the frozen lab, exact same-set GRU/transformer matches (same optimizer, lr, budget, loss and d_model) are only 4 pairs: two 512-step d64 pairs with opposite winners, and two 256-step d32 pairs where the transformer wins. Exact transformer FF-width matches are 3 pairs in one set: d_ff=256 beats 64 and 128, while 128 beats 64. Exact one-vs-two-layer GRU matches are 4 pairs: at Adam 3e-4/256, depth winners split by width; at AdamW .003/1024, one layer beats two at d64 (3.3353 vs 3.9614) and d32 (2.9757 vs 3.0719). These sparse, heterogeneous cells do not identify a universal ordering.

Competing explanations. Narrow FF may bottleneck token-to-logit mapping; learned positional/attention pathways may be optimization overhead at tiny lr; extra GRU depth may add a gated optimization bottleneck or history-fitting capacity. Initialization variance, beta2, decay coupling, batch/noise and finite reuse can mimic architecture effects. Endpoint losses do not identify mechanism.

Falsifiable prediction. On one fresh allowed set, d_model=64, AdamW 3e-5, matched CE/initialization and 2048 steps, compare transformer d_ff/d_model in {1,2,4} and GRU depth in {1,2}. Predict an interaction: increasing FF ratio lowers CE more for 2 transformer layers than 1; a 1-layer GRU may beat ratio-1/2 transformers but not ratio-4, while a 2-layer GRU should lose to ratio-4 and show the largest gap. Failure weakens the FF-by-depth account.

Boundary and uncertainty. Do not pool across width, FF ratio, depth, data law, optimizer/decay, initialization or budget. Do not infer universal transformer preference, universal high-rate punishment, or an alpha cutoff. Seed-level covariance, trajectories, and causal separation of FF width from recurrent depth remain unresolved.
