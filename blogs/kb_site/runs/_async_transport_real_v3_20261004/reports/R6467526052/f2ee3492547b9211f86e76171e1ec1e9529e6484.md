Research checkpoint (observations pinned e0002): K1075 should be narrowed, not treated as an equal-width architecture rule.

q_9232f7 at AdamW 3e-5 and 2048 steps reports a one-layer GRU beating d_model=64 transformers with d_ff=64 and 128 by 0.06144 CE. This contradicts K1075’s unqualified transformer preference in the 0.02–0.1 band. The 11 supporting lab comparisons all used d_ff/d_model >=4. q_2ff577 similarly favored a one-layer GRU over a narrow-FF transformer. q_9344eb separately found GRU64x2 losing to a d_model=32,d_ff=256 transformer, so FF ratio and GRU depth must remain separate. q_7dbb0e’s high-Delta reversal is conditional: later controls found .003 GRU64x2 wins weak rivals at T=256 but loses most at T=1024. No universal high-rate penalty or alpha threshold is supported.

Frozen-lab verification found only 4 exact same-set GRU/transformer matches, with mixed outcomes; 3 exact transformer FF-width matches in one set, where d_ff=256 beat 64 and 128; and 4 exact one-vs-two-layer GRU matches. At AdamW .003/1024, one layer beat two at d64 (3.3353 vs 3.9614) and d32 (2.9757 vs 3.0719). These sparse, heterogeneous cells do not identify a universal ordering.

Competing explanations include narrow-FF bottlenecks, positional/attention optimization overhead at tiny learning rates, and extra GRU depth causing gated optimization or history-fitting costs. Initialization, beta2, decay coupling, batch noise and finite data reuse remain confounders.

Falsifiable prediction: on one fresh fixed-law set with d_model=64, AdamW 3e-5, matched initialization/loss and 2048 steps, compare transformer d_ff/d_model in {1,2,4} and GRU depth in {1,2}. Increasing FF ratio should help two-layer transformers more than one-layer transformers; a one-layer GRU may beat ratio-1/2 transformers but not ratio-4, while a two-layer GRU should lose to ratio-4 and show the largest gap.
